Offer & Counteroffer Templates

Complete Course32 min readModule 12 of 12

Templates save time, reduce errors, and ensure you don't miss anything important under the pressure of a live negotiation. This module walks you through how purchase offer forms work, provides a line-by-line explanation of a standard offer, and gives you ready-to-adapt templates for the most common FSBO scenarios: the single offer, the counteroffer, the multiple-offer response, the inspection repair request response, and the rejection letter. These templates are starting points — always have a real estate attorney review documents before signing in your specific transaction.

Templates That Protect Your Position

These are the documents agents use to structure transactions — the same ones buyers and their agents are trained to read carefully. Having professional templates gives you credibility, speed, and legal protection at every step.

How Purchase Offer Forms Work

When a buyer's agent submits an offer, they typically use their state's standard residential purchase agreement — the same form we discussed in Module 5. When an unrepresented buyer (one without an agent) submits an offer to an FSBO seller, they may:

  • Use the state standard form if they obtained it themselves
  • Submit a letter of intent that outlines terms, which you then formalize on the standard form
  • Ask you for a contract template — in which case you can use your state's standard form or a form your real estate attorney provides

Do not use online "generic" purchase contract templates that aren't specific to your state. Real estate contracts are heavily regulated by state law, and a non-compliant form creates legal risk. Use your state's standard form — available from your state's real estate commission or a local real estate attorney.

Line-by-Line: Reading a Standard Offer

Here are the key fields you'll encounter in any standard residential purchase offer and what to look for in each:

FieldWhat to Look ForRed Flags
Purchase PriceThe offered amountMore than 5–7% below list price without justification
Earnest MoneyAmount and deposit deadlineLess than 1% of purchase price; extended deposit deadline
Financing TypeConventional, FHA, VA, CashFHA/VA with low down payment on a home with condition issues
Down Payment %Buyer's equity in the dealUnder 5% — buyer is more likely to walk if issues arise
Loan AmountShould match purchase price minus down paymentInconsistency between purchase price, down payment, and loan amount
Closing DateProposed closing timelineLess than 25 days for financed offer (typically not achievable)
Inspection ContingencyNumber of days for inspectionMore than 14 days is unusual; no inspection contingency is favorable to you
Financing ContingencyNumber of days to secure loanMore than 30 days; vague language about loan terms
Appraisal ContingencyWaived or in placeIn place at a price significantly above your comp range
Closing Costs RequestedSeller contribution to buyer's costsMore than 3% of purchase price in a normal market
Personal PropertyWhat the buyer expects includedItems not normally included (furniture, décor)

Template 1: Acceptance Letter (Full Price, No Changes)

When you receive an offer that meets your terms and you want to accept it as written:

ACCEPTANCE LETTER TEMPLATE

Date: [DATE]

To: [BUYER NAME] / [BUYER'S AGENT NAME if applicable]

Re: Acceptance of Purchase Offer — [PROPERTY ADDRESS]

Dear [Buyer Name],

We are pleased to confirm our acceptance of your purchase offer dated [DATE] for the property located at [ADDRESS] at the offered purchase price of $[AMOUNT] under the terms as submitted.

Enclosed/attached is the fully executed Purchase and Sale Agreement. Please confirm receipt and ensure the earnest money deposit of $[AMOUNT] is delivered to [TITLE COMPANY NAME] by [DATE] per the contract terms.

We look forward to a smooth transaction and wish you well in your new home.

Sincerely,
[YOUR NAME]
Owner-Seller | [PROPERTY ADDRESS]
[YOUR PHONE] | [YOUR EMAIL]

Template 2: Counteroffer Letter

When you want to modify the buyer's offer on one or more terms:

COUNTEROFFER TEMPLATE

Date: [DATE]

To: [BUYER NAME] / [BUYER'S AGENT NAME if applicable]

Re: Seller's Counteroffer — [PROPERTY ADDRESS]

Dear [Buyer Name],

Thank you for your offer dated [DATE] for the above-referenced property. After careful review, we respectfully submit the following counteroffer. All terms of your original offer remain in effect except as modified below:

1. Purchase Price: $[YOUR COUNTER PRICE] (modified from $[BUYER'S OFFERED PRICE])
2. Closing Date: [YOUR PREFERRED DATE] (modified from [BUYER'S PROPOSED DATE])
3. Earnest Money: $[YOUR REQUESTED AMOUNT] to be deposited within [X] business days of acceptance
4. Seller Closing Cost Contribution: [State your position — e.g., "Seller will contribute $0 toward buyer's closing costs" or "Seller will contribute up to $X"]
5. [Any additional modifications]

This counteroffer expires at [TIME] on [DATE]. Please confirm your acceptance or response in writing prior to this deadline.

We remain committed to working toward a mutually beneficial agreement and look forward to your response.

Sincerely,
[YOUR NAME]
Owner-Seller | [PROPERTY ADDRESS]
[YOUR PHONE] | [YOUR EMAIL]

Template 3: Multiple Offer Notification

When you've received more than one offer and want to invite best-and-final submissions:

MULTIPLE OFFER NOTIFICATION TEMPLATE

Date: [DATE]

To: [BUYER NAME] / [BUYER'S AGENT NAME]

Re: Multiple Offer Situation — [PROPERTY ADDRESS]

Dear [Buyer Name],

Thank you for your interest in [PROPERTY ADDRESS]. We want to inform you that we have received multiple purchase offers on this property.

We are requesting that all interested buyers submit their highest and best offer by [TIME] on [DATE]. Please ensure your best offer addresses: purchase price, earnest money, financing terms, contingencies, closing date, and any other terms important to your offer.

We will review all offers received by the deadline and respond to the selected buyer within [X] hours/business days.

We appreciate your continued interest and look forward to reviewing your best offer.

Sincerely,
[YOUR NAME]
Owner-Seller | [PROPERTY ADDRESS]

Template 4: Inspection Response — Accepting Requests

INSPECTION RESPONSE — ACCEPTING TEMPLATE

Date: [DATE]

To: [BUYER NAME] / [BUYER'S AGENT NAME]

Re: Inspection Response — [PROPERTY ADDRESS]

Dear [Buyer Name],

Thank you for submitting your inspection response dated [DATE]. After careful review, we respond as follows:

Items We Agree To Address:
• [ITEM 1]: Seller will [repair using licensed contractor / provide closing credit of $X]
• [ITEM 2]: Seller will [repair using licensed contractor / provide closing credit of $X]

Items We Decline to Address:
• [ITEM 3]: [Brief, professional explanation — e.g., "This item was disclosed in our seller disclosure and is reflected in the purchase price."]
• [ITEM 4]: [e.g., "This is normal wear consistent with the age of the home and was visible during the buyer's inspection period."]

The above represents our complete response to the inspection request. We remain committed to closing on [CLOSING DATE] and look forward to your confirmation to proceed.

Sincerely,
[YOUR NAME]

Template 5: Rejection Letter

When you're declining an offer outright (no counteroffer):

REJECTION LETTER TEMPLATE

Date: [DATE]

To: [BUYER NAME] / [BUYER'S AGENT NAME]

Re: Response to Purchase Offer — [PROPERTY ADDRESS]

Dear [Buyer Name],

Thank you for your offer dated [DATE] for [PROPERTY ADDRESS]. After careful consideration, we have decided to decline this offer at this time.

We appreciate your interest in our home and wish you the best in your search.

Sincerely,
[YOUR NAME]
Owner-Seller | [PROPERTY ADDRESS]

Keep All Communications Professional and Brief

Rejection letters and counteroffers should never include emotional commentary, explanations of why you're insulted, or personal reactions to the offer. Keep all written communications factual, brief, and professional. You may encounter this buyer again — in a different market, at a different price — and bridges should never be burned unnecessarily.

The Post-Acceptance Checklist

After you've accepted an offer, send the buyer (or their agent) a confirmation package that includes:

  • Fully executed purchase agreement (signed by all parties)
  • Your seller disclosure form(s)
  • Lead paint disclosure (if applicable)
  • HOA documents (if applicable)
  • Title company contact information and escrow instructions
  • Showing schedule cancellation notice (the home is under contract — cancel any pending showings)
  • Your direct contact information for transaction coordination

Template 6: Repair Credit vs. Price Reduction Response Letter

When a buyer requests a post-inspection price reduction (rather than a credit), you need a specific response that redirects them to a credit structure. The economics are identical for the buyer, but the mechanics matter to you: a credit preserves the purchase price (and appraised value), while a price reduction directly affects the comp value your sale establishes for the neighborhood. Never concede a price reduction when a credit achieves the same outcome for the buyer.

The framing of your response is everything. You're not refusing their request — you're proposing a structurally cleaner solution that achieves exactly what they asked for:

REPAIR CREDIT vs. PRICE REDUCTION RESPONSE TEMPLATE

Date: [DATE]

To: [BUYER NAME] / [BUYER'S AGENT NAME]

Re: Inspection Response — [PROPERTY ADDRESS]

Dear [Buyer Name],

We appreciate your inspection response and understand your concerns about [specific items noted in the inspection]. After consulting with licensed contractors and reviewing the findings carefully, we propose addressing these items through a closing cost credit rather than a purchase price adjustment.

A closing credit of $[AMOUNT] achieves the same economic outcome for you — putting the same dollars toward your repair costs after closing — while maintaining the purchase price and appraised value of the property. This structure is cleaner for both your lender and the title company, as it avoids the need to re-underwrite the loan at a reduced price and preserves the appraised value that your lender has already accepted.

We believe this is the most straightforward path to closing on schedule. Please confirm your agreement so we can proceed.

Sincerely,
[YOUR NAME]
Owner-Seller | [PROPERTY ADDRESS]
[YOUR PHONE] | [YOUR EMAIL]

Why FSBO sellers should always prefer credits over price reductions in inspection negotiations: beyond the appraisal and comp impact, a price reduction requires an amendment to the purchase contract that all parties must re-sign — introducing delay and reopening the conversation in a way that a closing credit does not. A credit is simply added to the closing statement. It is faster, simpler, and cleaner for everyone involved.

Template 7: Post-Closing Occupancy (Rent-Back) Agreement

A post-closing occupancy agreement — also called a seller rent-back or leaseback — allows you to remain in the home as a tenant after the buyer takes legal ownership at closing. This is an increasingly common arrangement in markets where sellers need time to find replacement housing, and it can be negotiated as a standalone term in your counteroffer or as a separate addendum. It eliminates the need for interim housing and can save you thousands in hotel, storage, and moving costs — making it a legitimate negotiating chip with real dollar value.

Before any rent-back agreement, have your real estate attorney review the final document. The following framework covers the essential terms:

POST-CLOSING OCCUPANCY AGREEMENT — KEY TERMS

Property: [Full property address]
Seller/Occupant: [Your name]
Buyer/Owner: [Buyer name]

Occupancy Period: Seller shall have the right to occupy the Property from the closing date of [DATE] through [POSSESSION DATE], a period of [X] days.

Daily Rental Rate: $[AMOUNT] per day, calculated based on Buyer's daily PITI payment (principal, interest, taxes, insurance) of $[BUYER DAILY PITI]. [Note: rental rate is typically set at buyer's actual daily carrying cost — this protects the buyer from subsidizing your occupancy while not overcharging you.] Total rent for occupancy period: $[TOTAL].

Security Deposit: $[AMOUNT] (equal to [X] weeks of daily rental rate), held by [TITLE COMPANY NAME] and returned to Seller within [X] days of vacating, less any documented damages.

Utilities: Seller is responsible for all utility costs during the occupancy period.

Maintenance: Seller agrees to maintain the Property in substantially the same condition as at closing. Buyer's homeowner's insurance takes effect at closing; Seller shall maintain renter's insurance covering Seller's personal property and liability during the occupancy period.

Holdover Penalty: If Seller fails to vacate by [POSSESSION DATE], a holdover penalty of $[DAILY PENALTY — typically 1.5x–2x the daily rental rate] per day applies until Seller vacates and delivers possession to Buyer.

Condition at Vacancy: Seller shall deliver the Property in broom-clean condition, free of personal property, on or before the possession date.

[SIGNATURES OF ALL PARTIES AND DATE]

Template 8: "Back on Market" Notification

When a transaction falls apart after going under contract and your home returns to active status, the first 24–48 hours of communication are critical. Every agent who showed your home during the previous listing period is asking the same question: what happened? If you don't control the narrative with a clear, professional, brief explanation, speculation fills the void — and speculation in real estate is almost always more alarming than the actual truth.

Send the following letter via email to every agent who requested or completed a showing during your listing period. Pull this list from your ShowingTime records or showing request history before you go back on market:

BACK ON MARKET NOTIFICATION TEMPLATE

Subject: Back on Market: [PROPERTY ADDRESS]

Date: [DATE]

Dear [AGENT NAME / "To Interested Parties"],

We wanted to let you know that [PROPERTY ADDRESS] is back on the active market as of today.

The previous transaction did not proceed due to [choose the accurate description: buyer financing / an inspection negotiation that could not be resolved / buyer's personal circumstances unrelated to the property condition]. The property itself remains in excellent condition, and we remain motivated sellers.

We welcome showings immediately and are available to discuss any questions about the property or our terms. Please reach out to schedule a tour at your earliest convenience.

Sincerely,
[YOUR NAME]
Owner-Seller | [PROPERTY ADDRESS]
[YOUR PHONE] | [YOUR EMAIL]

After sending this letter, update your MLS listing status to "Back on Market" (BOM). Many MLS systems reset the days-on-market counter when a listing is marked BOM — not all systems do this automatically, so check with your flat-fee MLS provider. A reset DOM counter is a legitimate advantage: buyers and agents who see "3 days on market" on a BOM listing treat it with fresh-listing energy, not the skepticism that accompanies a 45-day-on-market history.

Building Your Offer Comparison Spreadsheet

When one offer arrives, it's tempting to simply evaluate it in isolation and respond. When two or more offers arrive, evaluating them without a structured comparison framework is how sellers make decisions they later regret. The offer that looks best at first glance — the highest headline number — is frequently not the best offer when you account for concessions, contingency risk, financing type, and timeline. A pre-built comparison spreadsheet removes emotion and lets the math make the decision for you.

Build this spreadsheet before your listing goes live. Have it ready so that when offers arrive — even at 9pm on a Saturday — you can populate it immediately and see the complete picture within minutes.

FieldOffer A (Example)Offer B (Example)
Buyer / Agent NameJohnson / Smith RealtyWilliams / Unrepresented
Purchase Price$430,000$420,000
Earnest Money$5,000 (1.2%)$10,000 (2.4%)
Financing TypeConventionalCash
Down Payment %10%N/A (cash)
Appraisal ContingencyYes — standardWaived
Inspection Contingency10 days5 days
Financing Contingency21 daysNone (cash)
Closing Cost Contribution Requested$8,000$0
Closing Date45 days21 days
Possession DateDay of closingDay of closing
Special TermsWasher/dryer includedNone
Estimated Seller Closing Costs$3,800$3,800
Estimated Net to Seller$418,200$416,200
Deal Risk AssessmentModerate — appraisal contingency + financingVery low — cash, no appraisal, minimal contingencies

In the example above, Offer A has a $10,000 higher purchase price — but after the $8,000 closing cost request, it nets only $2,000 more than Offer B. And Offer B is cash with a waived appraisal contingency, a 21-day close, and minimal contingencies. A transaction that closes in 21 days with no appraisal risk versus a 45-day transaction with appraisal and financing exposure is not a close comparison — Offer B is almost certainly the better deal despite the lower headline price.

Sort your comparison sheet by "Estimated Net to Seller" — not by purchase price. This is the column that tells you the truth about what each offer actually delivers. The exercise of building the spreadsheet also documents your decision-making process, which matters for a different reason:

Save Every Comparison Sheet

If you ever receive more than one offer and don't accept the highest headline price, there is a small but real chance a declined buyer challenges your decision — especially in commercial transactions or if a buyer believes discrimination played a role. A completed, dated offer comparison spreadsheet showing your documented methodology — net proceeds, risk assessment, timeline — is your evidence that the decision was based entirely on financial and contractual merit. Keep every completed comparison sheet in your transaction file through closing and for at least 12 months afterward.

Key Takeaways

  • Always use your state's standard purchase contract form — generic online templates create legal risk
  • Evaluate every offer across the full table: price, earnest money, financing type, contingencies, closing costs, and timeline
  • All counteroffers should be in writing, address all modifications simultaneously, and include an expiration deadline
  • Multiple-offer notification should go to all buyers simultaneously — never share one buyer's terms with another
  • Inspection responses should address each requested item explicitly — accept, counter with credit, or decline with brief professional language
  • Keep all written communications brief, professional, and emotion-free — they become part of the transaction record
  • Always redirect price reduction requests to closing credits — same economic result for the buyer, better outcome for your appraisal comp and neighborhood comps
  • Rent-back agreements have significant legal exposure — have your attorney review any post-closing occupancy document before signing
  • Send a proactive "Back on Market" notification to all previous showing agents immediately — controlling the narrative prevents speculation that depresses your relisting price
  • Sort your offer comparison spreadsheet by Net to Seller, not Purchase Price — and save every completed comparison sheet for at least 12 months post-closing
